# Bangladesh Innovation Fair 2026 fit research

Research date: 2 September 2026. Sources are limited to the fair's own website and first-party government, UN, and World Bank publications.

## Bottom line

Digital Delta is a strong thematic fit for Bangladesh Innovation Fair 2026. The fair has categories for "Climate, Environment & Sustainability," "Information & Digital Technology," "Artificial Intelligence, Automation & Robotics," and "Frontier Technology." It also lists individuals, students, and researchers among the participant groups. The best main category is **Climate, Environment & Sustainability**, because that makes the public problem clear before the technical explanation starts. The software, routing, cryptography, and predictive model can sit underneath that story. [Official fair homepage](https://innovationfairbd.org/)

The idea should not be presented as the original eight-module hackathon specification. That version is impressive but too broad for one person and too hard to prove at a booth. The fair asks entrants to address practical impact, commercialization, technical capability, sustainable development, inclusion, safety, and market demand, as well as originality. A smaller system that completes one relief mission offline will answer those questions better than eight half-working modules. [Official innovator application](https://innovationfairbd.org/innovator-application-form?type=innovator)

One immediate obstacle matters. The official innovator application currently says that registration has closed. The page does not publish the closing date or a late-submission process. The user should contact the organizers now, before building around an assumed place. The website lists Novo Theatre, Dhaka, info@innovationfairbd.org, and +8801920376729. [Official homepage](https://innovationfairbd.org/) [Official application](https://innovationfairbd.org/innovator-application-form?type=innovator)

## Disaster claims that can be defended

The problem is real. A few numbers in the pasted hackathon brief are not.

- Bangladesh's Department of Disaster Management identifies Sylhet and Sunamganj as haor and flash-flood areas. Its risk table rates Sunamganj's overall risk "Very High" and Sylhet's "High." [DDM risk information](https://rapid.ddm.gov.bd/risk/riskinfo)
- A government and humanitarian-partner rapid assessment of the June 2024 northeastern floods recorded **3,742,924 affected people across six assessed districts**, including 852,357 in Sylhet, 847,696 in Sunamganj, and 456,939 in Netrokona. [UN Bangladesh-hosted NAWG rapid assessment](https://bangladesh.un.org/en/272834-rapid-assessment-northeastern-flood-2024-needs-assessment-working-group-nawg-28-june-2024)
- During the June 2022 flash floods, a UN situation update reported an estimated **7.2 million affected people in nine northeastern districts**, 472,856 people taken to about 1,605 shelters, isolated households, and power cuts. [UN Bangladesh situation update](https://bangladesh.un.org/en/187390-severe-flash-floods-situation-update-2-22-june-2022)
- A UNFPA account from Sunamganj says one hospital sheltered about 25,000 people and its midwives worked for the first three days without electricity or a functioning telecommunications network. That is direct support for offline-capable field coordination. [UN Bangladesh account](https://bangladesh.un.org/en/195588-pregnant-midwife-serves-pregnant-mothers-during-sylhet-floods)
- The World Bank's 2025 B-STRONG flood-recovery programme covers Sylhet Division and includes roads, bridges, shelters, flood forecasting, boats, equipment, training, and drills. This supports the broader claim that resilient transport and local disaster-response capacity are active public needs. [World Bank B-STRONG announcement](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2025/05/14/world-bank-supports-bangladesh-in-flood-risk-reduction-and-recovery)

The statement that "in July 2025, Sylhet, Sunamganj, and Netrokona recorded over 5.2 million displaced persons within 72 hours" could not be verified in official sources and appears to mix up another event. Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha, citing the Disaster Management and Relief Ministry, reported **5,209,798 people affected**, not displaced, in **11 districts** during the August 2024 eastern floods. It reported 415,273 people in shelters. UNICEF's first situation report for the same flood gave about five million affected and 284,888 people in shelters at its reporting point. [Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha report](https://www.bssnews.net/news-flash/205884) [UNICEF situation report](https://www.unicef.org/media/160746/file/Bangladesh-Situation-Report-No.1)

The pitch should therefore remove the July 2025, three-district, 5.2-million-displaced claim. It should also avoid the unverified statement that cellular infrastructure, power, and roads all collapsed across the whole region. A defensible version is:

> Recurrent flash floods in northeastern Bangladesh isolate communities and disrupt power, telecommunications, roads, health services, and relief delivery. Digital Delta keeps essential requests, routing decisions, and delivery proof working when field teams lose internet connectivity.

## Recommended fair version of Digital Delta

### Product claim

"Digital Delta is an offline relief dispatch system that carries a verified medical request from an isolated clinic to the right boat or drone, reroutes around a failed path, and records delivery without relying on the internet."

That sentence is narrower than the hackathon statement, but much easier to prove.

### The demo that would make the idea credible

Use three visible roles: a clinic phone, a volunteer or boat phone, and a command dashboard. Start with internet disabled.

1. The clinic creates a P0 medical request.
2. A nearby phone relays it through local peer-to-peer transfer or a clearly labelled simulated mesh if true device-to-device radio is not ready.
3. The dashboard chooses a boat-compatible route.
4. The presenter marks one edge flooded. The route changes immediately and shows the reason.
5. At delivery, the two devices exchange and verify a signed QR code.
6. The receipt appears in the local chain of custody, then syncs when connectivity returns.

This sequence proves offline operation, routing, priority, security, and recovery in one story. Predictive route risk, CRDT conflict handling, battery throttling, and drone handoff can appear as follow-on capabilities. They should enter the live demo only after the main path works reliably.

### What "awesome enough" means for this fair

The site is built around taking innovations to market, not around maximizing a hackathon module count. A competitive entry needs more than a dramatic map:

- a real offline run, with airplane mode or the network cable visibly removed;
- one measured result, such as reroute time, message-delivery success across disconnects, or memory use;
- a named first user and buyer hypothesis, such as a district disaster office, response NGO, or hospital network;
- a realistic deployment kit and cost estimate;
- a safety boundary explaining that the system supports human dispatchers and does not replace official evacuation or medical judgement;
- Bangla-first field screens, large controls, readable status, and an audit trail that a judge can inspect.

My assessment is that this can do well if the prototype is honest and the offline path actually works. It is less likely to stand out if it is shown as a dashboard fed by the supplied Flask chaos server, because that proves online polling and visualization, not disaster-resilient coordination. The winning move is not more modules. It is one complete handoff under a deliberate network failure, backed by defensible Bangladesh evidence and a believable adoption plan.
